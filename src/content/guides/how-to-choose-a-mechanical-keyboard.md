Mechanical keyboard shopping is a rabbit hole of switches, keycaps, and gasket mounts. Before any of that, three practical filters will eliminate most of the market.

## Step 1: Confirm compatibility with your setup

Check the layout against your OS, ports, and desk. Windows and Mac handle keys differently — confirm the board supports your platform (and wireless dongle slot if you need one). Measure your desk: a 75% or TKL may fit where a full-size won't.

## Step 2: Count the exact ports and inputs you need

List what you plug in daily: USB-C, passthrough ports, or none. If the board has a USB hub, confirm it can power what you need. Wireless users should check battery life and whether the dongle stores inside the board for travel.

## Step 3: Judge build quality from real owner reviews

Skip marketing photos. Search owner reviews for specific complaints — rattly stabilizers, PCB flex, keycap quality, software bugs. Consistent complaints across many owners are the signal; one-off rants are noise.

## Step 4: Check the warranty and return window

Keyboards fail in the first year more often than any other peripheral. A one-year warranty and a no-questions return window protect you from a board that sounds great in reviews but ships with a dead switch row.
