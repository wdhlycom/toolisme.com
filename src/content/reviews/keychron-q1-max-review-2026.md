## Overview

The Keychron Q1 Max is the wireless evolution of the Q1, a keyboard that already had a cult following among mechanical keyboard enthusiasts. The Max version adds Bluetooth and 2.4GHz wireless, keeps the CNC aluminum chassis, and retains full QMK/VIA programmability. At $179, it sits in the premium bracket — but the build quality justifies the price the moment you pick it up.

We tested the Q1 Max with Brown Gateron switches for three weeks of daily development work — TypeScript, Python, and a lot of Markdown. The gasket mount combined with the foam layers underneath produced a typing sound that was deep and muted, with no pinging or metallic resonance. It is the kind of sound that makes you want to type more.

## Key features

The gasket-mounted plate is the heart of the Q1 Max's typing feel. The plate is suspended by gaskets between the top and bottom case halves, which gives the board a slight bounce on bottom-out. Combined with the PORON foam and the tape mod from the factory, the result is a cushioned, quiet typing experience that is noticeably more comfortable over long sessions than a tray-mounted board.

QMK/VIA support means you can remap every key, set up layers, and create macros directly in the browser-based VIA app — no firmware flashing required. We set up a layer with arrow keys on IJKL, media controls on the function row, and a macro for our most-used shell command. The changes took effect instantly.

Tri-mode wireless is the headline addition over the original Q1. Bluetooth 5.1 lets you pair up to three devices and switch between them with a key combination. The 2.4GHz dongle provides a lower-latency connection for when you need it. USB-C wired mode charges the 4000mAh battery while you type. In our testing, Bluetooth was rock-solid for coding work; we only reached for the dongle for gaming.

## Pricing

The Q1 Max starts at $179 for the barebone kit (no switches or keycaps) and $199 fully assembled with Gateron Brown or Red switches. For comparison, the Keychron V1 Max (plastic chassis) is $79 — less than half the price. The Q1 Max's premium buys you the aluminum frame, gasket mount, and better acoustic dampening.

For a tool you use 8 hours a day, 250 days a year, the $199 asking price works out to about $0.10 per hour of use in the first year alone. That is a rounding error compared to the productivity gain from a keyboard that feels this good to type on.

## Verdict

The Keychron Q1 Max is not a budget keyboard, and it is not trying to be. It is a premium tool for people who treat their keyboard the way a chef treats their knives — as something they interact with constantly and want to enjoy using. For developers, writers, and anyone who spends most of their workday typing, the Q1 Max delivers a typing experience that cheaper boards simply cannot match. If you are on a tight budget, the V1 Max gets you 80% of the way for half the price. But if you want the best, this is it.
