# Buffer Review: From "Posting Every Day" to "Queueing Once a Week"

Last week I helped a friend with her indie shop's social accounts. Instagram, X, LinkedIn — three logins, and she was cycling through them every morning just to post. That ritual ate the first 40 minutes of her day.

I told her: get a scheduler. Her first reaction was "Hootsuite? Isn't that for big companies? Expensive and complicated."

I said: try Buffer. Ten minutes to set up, and the free plan is enough to start.

Now her mornings look different. She spends fifteen minutes dropping content into the queue, then closes the tab. Buffer handles the rest.

![Buffer dashboard — every platform in one place](/reviews/buffer-dashboard.png)

## Quick Verdict

Buffer's core value in one sentence: **it's the easiest multi-platform scheduler you can buy.** Connect 11+ platforms, queue a week of posts, see what grew — three steps, nothing else in the way.

Its strength is that it's *less*. No workflow maze, no feature wall. Across 1,000+ G2 reviews, the most repeated phrase is "almost no learning curve."

Its weakness is also that it's *less*. No social listening, no competitor tracking, no cross-channel benchmarks — that's Hootsuite and Sprout Social's territory. **It fits solo creators and small teams. It doesn't fit operators who live on deep data.**

## Who Is Buffer? A 2010 "Less-Is-More" Veteran

Buffer launched in 2010, founded by Joel Gascoigne and Leo Widrich with "simple" as the whole pitch. It grew to 1M+ users and 80+ employees, **bootstrapped all the way** — no VC-fueled feature bloat. Rare in the social-tools space.

Marketing folks know it for another reason: it's the most-cited scheduler in SERP roundups, appearing in all five top inbound-marketing lists we checked. In the "multi-platform scheduling" niche, Buffer is the benchmark.

## Core Features: What It Actually Does

### 1. Schedule to 11+ Platforms From One Place (the whole point)

Instagram (feed, Reels, Stories), Facebook, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube (including Shorts), Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon, Google Business Profile.

**Our take after two weeks:** connecting Instagram, X and LinkedIn took under ten minutes. Drop a week of content into the queue, and it posts on schedule. The unified **content calendar** is the quiet hero — one view of what goes where and when, drag to reschedule.

![Buffer content calendar — drag-and-drop rescheduling](/reviews/buffer-calendar.png)

### 2. AI Assistant (unlimited on paid plans)

GPT-4-powered: write captions, summarize long posts, or rewrite one piece of copy to match each platform's tone — a formal paragraph becomes a snappy X short.

**Our take:** we fed it an 800-word product description and asked for an Instagram version. The output read like a person wrote it, not a translator. The standout part: **no credit system.** Paid plans use it unlimited. That's rare, and it's generous.

![Buffer AI assistant — one draft, rewritten per platform](/reviews/buffer-ai-assistant.png)

### 3. Analytics (the honest caveat)

Tracks engagement, reach, follower growth and best posting times per channel. Paid plans add unlimited history, performance overviews and custom views.

**Straight talk:** this is Buffer's weakest spot. It answers "how did this post do?" — not "what should I do next?". No cross-channel benchmarks, no competitor views, no audience breakdown. If those matter, you'll pair it with another tool.

### 4. Engagement Inbox

Reply to comments and mentions across platforms from one screen, without hopping logins.

### 5. Start Page (link-in-bio)

A free Linktree-style page for your Instagram or TikTok bio — one link, all your destinations.

## Pricing: Per Channel, Cheap to Start, Scales Up

Buffer charges **per connected channel, not per seat.** One channel, one bill. Five channels, five bills. That's a blessing at small scale and the thing that stings later.

| Plan | Price | What you get | Best for |
|------|-------|--------------|----------|
| **Free** | $0 | 3 channels, 10 scheduled posts each, AI assistant, 30-day insights | Trying it out, solo creators |
| **Essentials** | $6/channel/mo (or **$5/mo billed annually**) | Unlimited posts, unlimited AI, advanced analytics, engagement inbox | Solo creators & small brands taking it seriously |
| **Team** | $12/channel/mo (or **$10/mo billed annually**) | Everything in Essentials + unlimited members, approval flows, custom permissions, branded reports | Small teams |

**Details worth knowing:**
- **14-day free trial** on all paid plans. No card required. When it ends, you drop back to Free automatically — nothing is charged unless you choose to stay.
- **Billed annually saves ~20%** — two months effectively free.
- **Nonprofits get 50% off**.
- **10+ channels triggers volume pricing** — per-channel rate drops as you scale.
- **No PayPal** — cards only (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, JCB, Discover, Diners Club).

**The math we'd do:** stay free at 3 channels; at 4–5 channels, Essentials runs $24–30/mo monthly or $20–25/mo annual; past 6, do the math — at $40–50/mo, flat-rate rivals start to look good.

## Where It Stumbles: Real Complaints, Not Polished Over

Buffer's Trustpilot score is a rough **2.1/5 (93 reviews)** — a loud contrast to G2's 4.3/5. The negative threads cluster around:

1. **Failed or duplicate posts.** Trustpilot and recent Reddit threads (2024–2025) both mention scheduled posts that don't go out, or go out twice. Mostly intermittent — but if you're running fully hands-off, that stings.
2. **Accounts dropping.** Some users report connected accounts disconnecting and needing re-authorization.
3. **Slow support.** One of the most common Trustpilot complaints.
4. **Per-channel cost creep.** 8 channels = $40+/mo, 10 channels = $50+/mo. One G2 reviewer put it plainly: "once the bill crossed $40/mo, I switched to a flat-rate competitor."
5. **Shallow analytics.** Not enough for client-facing reporting.

> **Our two-week note:** on three channels, we never hit a failed post. But we only ran three — the more channels and the more you rely on full automation, the more you should verify your own risk. **For anything important, eyeball it after the scheduled time.**

## Who Should Buy, Who Should Skip

**Buy it if:** you're a solo creator, freelancer, or small brand; you want "set it once, post all month"; you don't want to learn a complex tool; you'd rather test on a free plan than commit a card.

**Skip it if:** you need listening / competitor tracking / deep reports; you manage 7+ channels (the bill will talk you out of it); your team needs serious approval flows and permissions; you need zero-touch full automation (Buffer has intermittent posting risk).

## The Verdict

Buffer is the minimalist benchmark in social scheduling. It took a genuinely messy job — publishing everywhere — and compressed it to three steps: **connect, queue, check the numbers.** For solo creators and small teams, it's the least painful place to start, and the free plan is honest enough to run for two weeks at zero cost.

It's not the tool you retire with. Shallow analytics, per-channel billing, no listening — at some scale, you'll outgrow it. Use it as the starting point, not the endpoint.
